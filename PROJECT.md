# MAIC — Multi-Agent Imagination Critic

Repository: `MAIC-Multi-Agent-Imagination-Critic`

## Goal

Predict a team's discounted future return for a given task from a short observed trajectory. Like a coach, infer how the team behaves, imagine plausible continuations, and make an informed forecast. Update that forecast as new real observations arrive.

The target is the return expected from **continuing the observed team policy**. Current task progress alone is insufficient. Policy optimization is outside the initial scope.

## Data and scope

- Available: approximately 10,000 trajectories across four formation-control settings, generated by good and bad policies, with rewards at every timestep.
- Task scope: multi-agent go-to-goal and formation control; predator–prey is a planned extension.
- First implementation: numerical states and logged actions; imagination is included from the start.
- Verify before implementation: logged action availability/format, state fields, controller IDs, episode boundaries, and termination versus time-limit truncation.
- No VLM or vision backbone is required initially.

## Architecture

```text
Observed state/action history + task specification
                    |
        Relational temporal encoder
                    |
        Scene state + policy context
                    |
        Sample predicted joint actions
                    |
        Predict next state, reward, termination
                    |
        Repeat for several imagined branches
                    |
        Discounted rewards + learned tail value
                    |
        Expected team return + uncertainty estimate
```

Use shared agent encoders with graph/set attention and temporal memory. Keep the physical scene representation separate from inferred policy behavior. Support variable team sizes using masks and explicit agent roles.

Predict coordinated joint actions rather than unrelated per-agent choices. Sample a consistent policy-context hypothesis per imagined branch; update its recurrent memory during rollout. Revise the policy-context belief using new real observations, not self-generated evidence.

## Task conditioning

Use a learned task embedding plus structured parameters:

| Task | Parameters |
| --- | --- |
| Go-to-goal | Assigned goals and collision constraints |
| Formation | Desired relative geometry, scale, and agent roles |
| Predator–prey | Team roles, capture rules, and opponent behavior assumptions |

Condition action prediction, rewards, termination, and value on the task. Share physical dynamics where the mechanics match. Task IDs alone do not describe unseen task configurations.

## Mathematical specification

### 1. Inputs and prediction target

Let $s_t$ be the recorded joint state of $N$ agents, $\mathbf a_t=(a_t^1,\ldots,a_t^N)$ their executed joint action, and $g$ the task identity and structured parameters. Transition $t$ contains $(s_t,\mathbf a_t,r_t,s_{t+1},d_t)$, where $r_t$ is the team reward and $d_t=1$ means **true termination after that transition**.

The input is a causal history, optionally limited to the last $K$ transitions:

$$
h_t=(s_0,\mathbf a_0,\ldots,s_{t-1},\mathbf a_{t-1},s_t).
$$

The desired output is the expected remaining return under continuation of the observed controller:

$$
V^{\mathrm{cont}}(h_t,g)=\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^k r_{t+k}
\;\middle|\;h_t,g,\text{ observed controller continues}
\right],\qquad 0\leq\gamma<1.
$$

Rewards after termination are zero. This is a continuation value, **not an optimal-policy value**. Neither future actions nor future outcomes enter the input history. Let $\mathcal D$ denote the recorded episode dataset; assume the controller is unchanged within each evaluated continuation.

### 2. Scene, task, and policy representations

$$
e_g=\operatorname{TaskEncoder}(g),\qquad
x_t=f_\omega(h_t,e_g),\qquad
c_t=E_\eta(h_t,e_g).
$$

$x_t$ represents the current scene/history; $c_t$ represents inferred controller behavior. Use deterministic $c_t$ initially. An optional extension samples controller hypotheses $c^{(m)}\sim q_\eta(c\mid h_t,e_g)$; keep each hypothesis fixed within its imagined branch. Imagined histories update $x$, but do not supply new evidence to $E_\eta$ or $q_\eta$ about the real controller.

### 3. Joint-action prediction

For discrete or discretized actions, one possible coordinated decoder is autoregressive across agents:

$$
\hat\pi_\theta(\mathbf a_t\mid x_t,c_t,e_g)
=\prod_{i=1}^{N}
\hat\pi_\theta(a_t^i\mid a_t^{<i},x_t,c_t,e_g).
$$

For training, sample an observed prefix ending at $t$ and later real continuation points $j\geq t$ from the same controller. Hold $c_t$ fixed while updating the scene/history $x_j$. The supervised loss is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{act}}
=-\mathbb E_{(t,j)\sim\mathcal D}\!\left[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log
\hat\pi_\theta(a_j^i\mid a_j^{<i},x_j,c_t,e_g)
\right].
$$

During training, earlier-agent actions $a_j^{<i}$ are logged actions; during imagination they are sampled actions. Inputs at $j$ contain no action at $j$ or later except the earlier-agent actions explicitly supplied to the autoregressive decoder. Mask padded agents. For continuous controls, replace categorical cross-entropy with a continuous-distribution negative log-likelihood unless controls are explicitly binned.

Train on good **and** bad controllers. This decoder predicts their behavior; it is not trained to maximize reward.

### 4. Dynamics, reward, and termination

For the first state-only implementation, assume the recorded joint state is sufficient for physical prediction:

$$
\hat s_{t+1}=s_t+F_\phi(s_t,\mathbf a_t),\qquad
\hat r_t=R_\rho(s_t,\mathbf a_t,\hat s_{t+1},e_g),\qquad
\hat p_t=D_\delta(s_t,\mathbf a_t,\hat s_{t+1},e_g).
$$

$\hat p_t$ is the probability of true termination. Include physical parameters when mechanics differ; condition dynamics on history if state is incomplete. Learn using real transitions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dyn}}&=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\|s_{t+1}-s_t-F_\phi(s_t,\mathbf a_t)\|^2,\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rew}}&=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[r_t-R_\rho(s_t,\mathbf a_t,s_{t+1},e_g)]^2,\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{term}}&=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\operatorname{BCE}\!\left(d_t,D_\delta(s_t,\mathbf a_t,s_{t+1},e_g)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Normalize state dimensions and add a short multi-step dynamics loss. If rewards are analytically available, they can replace the learned reward head during imagination.

### 5. Imagination-based critic

Initialize each branch with $\hat s_0=s_t$ and the observed history. At each imagined step, encode the updated history, sample a joint action, predict the next state/reward, and sample $\hat d_k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\hat p_k)$.

For a branch of horizon $H$, define survival weights $w_0=1$ and $w_{k+1}=w_k(1-\hat d_k)$. Then

$$
\hat G_t^{(m)}=
\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^k w_k^{(m)}\hat r_k^{(m)}
+\gamma^H w_H^{(m)}V_\psi(\hat x_H^{(m)},c_t,e_g),
\qquad
\hat V_{\mathrm{MAIC}}(h_t,g)=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}\hat G_t^{(m)}.
$$

Use $c^{(m)}$ in place of $c_t$ if controller hypotheses are sampled. $V_\psi$ estimates the return beyond the explicit imagination horizon. The reward on a terminating transition is included; rewards and bootstrap **after** termination are excluded. Sample plausible actions rather than selecting the highest-return branch.

### 6. Real-data anchors and joint training

For a recorded segment ending at state $s_\tau$, build a detached return target backward:

$$
Y_\tau=
\begin{cases}
0,&\text{true terminal boundary},\\
\operatorname{sg}[V_{\bar\psi}(x_\tau,c_t,e_g)],&\text{administrative truncation or segment cutoff},
\end{cases}
\qquad
Y_j=r_j+\gamma(1-d_j)Y_{j+1},\quad j=\tau-1,\ldots,t.
$$

$\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients; $\bar\psi$ denotes a lagged target network. Keep the prefix's policy context $c_t$ fixed when constructing its continuation targets. Complete terminal episodes give Monte Carlo return-to-go; shorter segments give aligned multi-step TD targets. If a deadline is part of the actual task, model it as a true terminal boundary and include remaining time in the state.

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tail}}=\mathbb E_{(t,j)\sim\mathcal D}[V_\psi(x_j,c_t,e_g)-Y_j]^2,
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{imag}}=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[\hat V_{\mathrm{MAIC}}(h_t,g)-Y_t]^2.
$$

$$
\mathcal L=
\lambda_a\mathcal L_{\mathrm{act}}+
\lambda_s\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dyn}}+
\lambda_r\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rew}}+
\lambda_d\mathcal L_{\mathrm{term}}+
\lambda_v\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tail}}+
\lambda_I\mathcal L_{\mathrm{imag}}.
$$

All weights are nonnegative hyperparameters, not yet selected. Train the architecture with imagination from the start. Categorical samples do not support ordinary pathwise gradients: train the action decoder through supervised cross-entropy, not through reward maximization. Bootstrap targets are detached. Initial implementation may freeze sampled trajectories for the forecast-loss backward pass; differentiating through continuous dynamics is an explicit later design choice.

Training continuation points with fixed $c_t$ teaches the action and tail-value heads to use an earlier inferred controller context, matching their use during imagination. Future rewards supervise predictions; they never enter the observed prefix.

### 7. Uncertainty

For ensemble member $\ell$, let $\mu_\ell=\operatorname{mean}_m\hat G^{(\ell,m)}$. Report the ensemble mean and inspect both sources of spread:

$$
\hat\mu=\operatorname{mean}_\ell\mu_\ell,\qquad
\hat\sigma^2=
\underbrace{\operatorname{mean}_\ell\operatorname{Var}_m(\hat G^{(\ell,m)})}_{\text{within-model rollout spread}}
+\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_\ell(\mu_\ell)}_{\text{between-model disagreement}}.
$$

These are predictive uncertainty estimates, not guaranteed confidence bounds. Action probabilities and return intervals require held-out calibration checks. A distributional tail-value head can additionally represent uncertainty beyond horizon $H$.

## Evaluation and open choices

Hold out complete episodes and controller checkpoints. Compare policies on matched initial conditions. Measure return error, policy-ranking accuracy, selection regret, and uncertainty coverage across prefix lengths and tasks.

Compare against a direct return critic; ablate policy context and imagination. These are comparisons, not prerequisites for building the full architecture.

Open choices: action representation, history length, imagination horizon, joint-action decoder, uncertainty method, and reward scaling across tasks. If reward formulas are known, compare analytic rewards on predicted states with learned reward heads.

Research focus: whether behavior inferred from short histories improves imagined-return forecasts for unseen team controllers. Multi-agent world models and policy-conditioned evaluation already exist; the specific contribution must be established through comparisons.
